**PHILADELPHIA** — A group of graduate city planning students successfully completed their studio's mandatory site visit with their instructor, Dr. Practicing, last Friday. They confirmed that the study area exists and remains consistent with its representation on Google Maps.

"We saw lots of buildings, trees, and falling leaves, because it's fall right now," said one student.

The three-hour site visit consisted of two hours and 30 minutes on the train, 15 minutes in an Uber, and the remaining 15 minutes taking pictures. Students observed streets, sidewalks, and many people rushing home from work. All of these scenes had already been meticulously examined through Google Street View.

Dr. Practicing advised his students to look up at the sky to see if it was going to rain and find a place to hide. While taking shelter, researchers confirmed the presence of local residents, although it remained unclear whether they qualified as stakeholders or were simply random transit riders waiting for the train to arrive. However, Dr. Practicing strongly argued that the train station operator, who was from Philadelphia, was not a stakeholder.

By the end of the visit, students had collected 247 photographs, purchased five coffees, and reached no agreement on how to move forward. Dr. Practicing suggested that they pursue transit-oriented development, although no train station existed within the study area's boundaries.

During a biweekly faculty meeting for the studio, Dr. Practicing reported to the chair, Dr. Claude, that the visit had been a great success. He described the visit as an essential component of professional planning education, allowing students to confirm that the site exists and that trains do not always arrive on time.

"There's simply no substitute for being there," Dr. Practicing explained.

Following the successful visit, students were assigned a 60-page comprehensive site analysis report addressing existing conditions, community needs, transportation accessibility, economic development opportunities, and long-term planning recommendations.

No further instructions were provided.

Asked what the group had learned from the experience, one student identified an important finding that had not appeared in the preliminary desktop analysis.

"The site was farther away than expected."
